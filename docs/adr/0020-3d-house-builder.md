# 20. 3D house builder

Status: accepted (2026-11-09), amended (2026-11-10, 2026-11-12, 2026-11-13, 2026-11-14, 2026-11-15)

## Context

The PV layout (ADR 17) is entered as numbers: planes, module counts, MPPT inputs. Homeowners think in roofs. Shading, optimizers and strings that span roof faces cannot be described as one plane per string. The owner wants to build the house, place modules like a PV planner, see each string's production in 3D, and make that live scene the dashboard.

## Decision

**What it is.** An editor for the geometry of the PV layout. The layout stays the source of truth; the form stays for anyone who does not want 3D. The admin edits; viewers, and the cloud web app later, see the scene read-only.

**Scene.** The scene is part of the PV layout, owned by the layout feature module (ADR 19). Saving it is one configuration change with an effective-from date. Planes and strings are derived from it and are not edited separately once a scene exists. Units are metres, y up, origin at the first block's corner; a compass dial rotates the scene to true north. Plane azimuths follow ADR 17. No map or satellite tiles: they would send the home's address to a tile service.

- **Blocks.** A rectangular footprint (width, depth, eave height, rotation) with one roof: flat, mono-pitch, gable or hip, at a pitch. A plant has several blocks. Dormers, larger L-shapes and free-form polygons wait. The compass dial's angle is the bearing of the scene's −z axis; a block's width runs along x and its depth along z from its corner, turned clockwise seen from above. A mono-pitch roof rises from the front eave (+z), a gable's ridge runs along the width, a hip's along the longer side.
  - **Overhang.** A block's roof reaches past its walls by an eave overhang (Traufe) and a verge overhang (Ortgang), in metres measured level. Its faces are the roof of the footprint widened by the overhang with the eaves lowered down the slope, so the ridge stays put: a mono-pitch or gable roof takes the eave overhang front and back and the verge overhang at the sides, a flat or hip roof the eave overhang all round. The walls stay under the roof as it meets them; carport posts too. The overhang shades and carries module grids like the rest of the face, and a face's grid and chimneys are placed from its widened outline. A block stored without one has none; the builder's new block starts with 0.5 m at the eaves and 0.3 m at the verges. A negative overhang is refused.
  - **Colours.** A block may carry a roof colour and a wall colour, `#rrggbb`, that tint its generated finishes; without one it keeps the finish's own. Swatches offer common finishes (roofs: Ziegelrot, Anthrazit, Schwarz, Braun; plaster: Weiß, Creme, Grau) beside a custom colour. Another format is refused.
  - **Roof chimneys.** A roof chimney is an upright box on one roof face of a block, named by block and face like a module grid: its centre across and up the face's outline, its level width across the slope and depth along the fall line, and its height above the roof where its upper side meets it; its top is level. It shades like any face, and no module may overlap its footprint. One without a positive size, on no face or not wholly on its face is refused.
  - **Roof windows.** A roof window lies flush in one roof face, named like a roof chimney: its centre across and up the face's outline, its width across the slope and its length up it. It is drawn as a framed glass pane and never shades; no module may overlap it. One without a positive size, on no face, not wholly on its face or overlapping a roof chimney or another roof window is refused.
- **Modules.** The admin defines the roofs first, then places modules freely on each roof face, like a PV planner. Each module has its own place and its own orientation, portrait or landscape; each face has an edge setback. Strings are painted onto modules and may span faces. Optimizers are set per module.
  - A face's module grid holds one module type. A module's place is its lower left corner across and up the face's outline, in metres, seen from the front; its footprint is the module type's size, its long side across the slope in landscape. A flat roof's across runs along the block's width. The grid's orientation is the one a new module takes.
  - No two modules may overlap, and none may cross the setback or overlap a roof chimney or roof window; the builder keeps them 2 cm apart.
  - A scene stored with a grid's rows and columns loads with each module at its cell's corner, in the grid's orientation, so nothing moves; it is saved with places.
  - The scene is stored with its layout version as one document in the `pv` crate's schema, in which a module names its string by id. Through the API a module names its string by position in the version's strings, so new strings need no id first.
  - The core derives each string's module count, optimizer count and plane, the plane of its first module, with the `pv` crate; the browser's numbers are not trusted. A painted string of another module type than its grid is refused.
  - A painted module keeps its place along its string's wiring. The string runs through its modules in that order; a module without a place follows them. A module wired into a string takes the place after the string's last; unwired, it loses its place.
- **Obstacles.** Opaque boxes (a neighbour, a wall), cylinders (a pole), trees (a trunk with a crown, broadleaf or conifer) and hedges (a straight row), each with position, size and height. A tree shades all year; leaf fall is not modelled.
  - A broadleaf's crown is a sphere whose top is the tree's height; a conifer's is a cone of the crown's radius from a fifth of the tree's height to its top. A trunk a tenth of the crown's radius carries either. A tree stored without a kind is broadleaf.
  - A hedge has a corner, a length along x, a depth along z, a height and a rotation, like a block's footprint, and shades as a box. It draws as foliage.
- **Garage.** A block with walls and any roof; it needs no kind of its own.
- **Surroundings.** Ground surfaces and fences are drawn and never shade; the `pv` crate stores them and ignores them for shading.
  - A *ground surface* is a rectangle on the plot with a footprint like a block's and one finish: lawn, paving, driveway, gravel or path. Later surfaces draw over earlier ones. Without any, the plot is lawn.
  - A *fence* is a polyline on the ground with a height and one finish: wood, metal or wall.
  - The `pv` crate places both on the ground for the browser to draw; a surface without a positive width and depth, or a fence with fewer than two points or no positive height, is refused. Each finish is drawn with generated materials.
- **Equipment.** Markers for inverters and batteries, each linked to its device and placed on a block's wall, inside or outside.
  - A marker names its device, its kind (inverter or battery, so a hybrid inverter may carry one of each), its block, the wall (front along +z, right along +x, back, left) and its distance from the wall's left end seen from outside. It hangs at 1.5 m, at most half the eave, 10 cm off the wall; its label's anchor floats 1 m above the block's highest point. The `pv` crate places it, so the browser and the core agree; a marker past its wall's ends or on no block is refused. A marker of a removed device shows nothing.
- **Openings.** Windows and doors in the blocks' walls. They are drawn and never shade; the `pv` crate stores them, fits them on their walls for the browser, and ignores them for shading.
  - An opening names its kind (window, French window, front door or garage door), its block, the wall like a marker, and the distance from the wall's left end to its own left edge seen from outside; it has a width, a height and a sill height, 0 when left out. One on no block or on a carport, without a positive size, past its wall's ends, reaching above the eave, a door off the ground, or sharing wall with an earlier opening is refused. A scene stored without openings loads unchanged.
  - *Look:* a frame and leaf 5 cm proud of the wall, from generated maps: a window's two casements with dark panes that mirror the sky, on a sill; a panelled front door under a glazed fanlight and a small canopy; a ribbed sectional garage door. The wall behind is not cut.
  - *Night:* the front door's fanlight and about three windows in five glow warm, each window chosen by where it sits, so the same ones glow on every draw and the house looks lived in. They come on across dusk with the night's light (Look). The glow is an emissive map, and the light on the surface below, the lawn or a lower roof under the window, is one decal for all of them: no light source is added. Nothing animates it, so the still tier shows it as it is.
  - *Editing:* an opening is added in the first free place on a wall, in its kind's usual size. Selected, it is outlined on its wall: dragging it slides it along the wall, an arrow at its right edge drags its width and one above it its height, in 10 cm steps, kept on the wall and under the eave; a drag onto another opening is not made. The templates start with a front door and windows.
- **Car places.** A parking footprint, optionally under a carport whose roof carries modules, linked to one EVCC loadpoint device (ADR 10). It shows the car while one is connected.
  - A carport is a block marked open: a post at each corner reaches up to its roof instead of walls, and its roof faces carry module grids and shade like any other. A car place stands under one by position, not by reference.
  - A place names its device, its corner, width, length and rotation like a block's footprint, a colour (`#rrggbb`), and an uploaded body by id. The `pv` crate stands the car at the place's centre, its nose along the place's −z, its label's anchor 2.5 m up; a place without a positive size, with another colour or an id no SHA-256 is refused.
- **Car body.** One default body ships: a low-poly mid-size sedan of about 4,000 triangles and under 50 KB, cut from "2024 Tesla Model 3 + Interior" by tonielpro520 (CC BY 4.0) without badges, interior or textures, and painted in the place's colour. The About page credits it. The admin may upload a GLB per car place instead: at most 20 MB, no external URIs, scaled to the place's length and set on the ground, stored in the data directory and included in backups.
  - An upload is admitted only as a glTF 2.0 binary container whose JSON names no `uri` anywhere, so every buffer and image is embedded; the cap holds while the body streams. It is kept as `cars/<sha256>.glb` in the data directory and named by that digest; backups (ADR 3) take the `cars` directory with the database. Viewers read a body; uploading is the admin's.

**Editing.** Undo and redo until save. Saving checks that modules sit inside their face's setback and overlap neither each other nor a roof chimney or roof window. Unassigned modules are shown and produce nothing. A string whose open-circuit voltage at −10 °C exceeds the inverter's maximum DC input voltage (ADR 5) raises a warning, never a refusal, because the plant may already exist. That voltage scales the datasheet's Voc by its voltage coefficient, as string sizing does. The layout's answer carries the warnings, for strings entered by form too. The browser runs the same checks through the `pv` crate before saving.

**Physics.** Shading is computed in the `pv` crate (ADR 17), per bypass-diode substring, against blocks with their overhangs, roof chimneys, obstacles and carport roofs; diffuse light stays unshaded. The `pv` crate also turns blocks, roofs and obstacles into the faces and solids the browser draws, so what is drawn is what shades: the forecast shades with each face as a fan of triangles and with the solids. The core's forecast and the browser run the same code, so the scene and the forecast never disagree. three.js only draws. Editing asks the crate too: it keeps a changed opening on its wall, finds a new one's place, keeps a dragged chimney on its face, finds the chimney a click strikes, and lays out the walls and chimney footings the handles stand on. The browser holds no copy of that geometry.

**Rendering.** three.js directly, without a Svelte wrapper. The scene is built by pure functions from the layout and drawn by one thin component. It needs WebGL2. It redraws only on a live tick or a camera move, never in a continuous loop. An orbit camera offers preset views (north, east, south, west, top, isometric) from one button on the canvas, whose menu is closed until asked for; each viewer's last view is kept in their browser. The `pv` crate reaches the browser through its own WASM glue crate (ADR 2). The admin edits at every width, the phone included: setup's layout step is the builder, and a homeowner may set up from a phone.

**Loading.** The dashboard never waits for the scene. three.js and the scene load after the dashboard is interactive: noise textures in a worker, the plant built in slices, shader programs compiled ahead, and the first frame drawn hidden. Meanwhile the hero shows the scene's own studio backdrop with a faint ground shadow; "3D-Ansicht lädt …" appears only after a second. Once the GPU has finished the first frame, the scene and its labels fade in over about 350 ms; the car loads after the house and fades in on its own. With 2D chosen, under the low-power switch or without WebGL2, three.js is never requested; a first switch from 2D keeps the flow until the scene can fade over it. A failed load leaves the 2D flow with "Erneut versuchen".

**Time slider.** A date and time slider moves the sun and the shadows. Per-string production comes from measured values or estimates for past dates, the forecast for today and the next two days, and a clear-sky run of the `pv` crate in the browser for any other date. Once readings exist, each MPPT input's learned correction and its residual over the last 30 days are shown in the inspector's Strings group, beside the strings they judge, so a misplaced face or a missing obstacle is found there and seen where it is drawn. The day is picked in a calendar and the time scrubs on a slider (ADR 21); both sit on one line. Nothing changes the scene automatically.

- A day with nothing stored, such as one before the first reading, runs the clear sky too. The browser's clear sky caps no MPPT input, as it reads no inverter profile. The location is the admin's (ADR 9), so for a viewer the sun stays put and no clear sky runs.
- `GET /api/v1/corrections`, read like history, returns each MPPT input's learned factors and its residual: measured against modelled energy in kWh over the whole hours of the last 30 days that are both modelled on archive weather and measured, short of the AC limit, the model uncorrected.
- The clear sky is Ineichen and Perez's at sea level under a Linke turbidity of 3, with still air at 20 °C: a plant stores no altitude, and a turbidity climatology is a 30 MB table. Golden values come from pvlib.

**Live dashboard.** When a scene exists, the dashboard opens on it, updated by live ticks (ADR 9): each string's power, tinted by its share of its own peak and labelled at its centre (estimates carry "≈"); each inverter's AC power; the battery's power, direction and SOC; the car's charging power and the vehicle's SOC when EVCC reports them; grid import or export and house consumption from the `plant` device. Equipment labels point at their markers with leader lines, readable through walls; without markers they have no line. The dashboard's camera is locked until its lock toggle is opened: a finger on the scene scrolls the page and taps chips, and neither a drag nor the wheel turns or zooms it. Leaving the dashboard locks it again; the builder's camera is never locked. The 2D power flow serves when there is no scene, no WebGL2 or the low-power switch is on, and a toggle switches between them per browser. An inverter's label shows its `inverter.power`, the AC output, and its `pv.power` only when the profile maps no `inverter.power`; the label and the inverter's detail sheet show the same figure. The scene's tints and labels come from the live values only; three.js loads when the scene is shown.

**Look.** An architectural visualisation that matches the SPA (ADR 21), in studio mode: the plot fades into the page background, with no visible horizon.
- **Materials:** physically based and generated in code: plaster walls, tiled or flat roofs, grass, paving, bark and leaves. No texture files ship.
  - *Weathering:* on the full motion tier each finish varies in colour and roughness at a scale of metres, from noise computed in its shader that never repeats; loose ground (lawn, gravel, driveway) also blends its map at a second, turned scale, and a wall darkens at its foot. It costs no pass and no texture, only shader arithmetic, which the lite and still tiers drop (ADR 21): they draw each finish as it is. No map holds anything coarser than its grain or its bond, so none shows its repeat as a grid on any tier or at any zoom.
  - *Roof trim:* a pitched roof carries ridge and hip caps and verge tiles in its colour, and zinc gutters along its eaves. The browser finds them on the crate's roof faces: where two pitched faces meet, a face's sloping outer edges, and its level lowest edge.
  - *Drawn detail never shades.* Trim, a module's frame and every other detail the browser adds are drawn only; the `pv` crate's faces and solids stay what shades. What lies on a surface that casts the same shadow (modules, ground surfaces, trim) casts none of its own.
- **Modules:** full black: black cells, backsheet and frames under glass with a specular strength of 0.12 and no clear coat. A module is a body 3.5 cm proud of its roof, its satin frame round the cells.
- **Light:** the sun at its real position for the plant and time, casting soft shadows, with a sky model as the environment. The builder's time slider moves it. Light never falls as the sun rises, and a sun on the horizon already reads as day: daylight there is the sky's, not the sun's. The sun's own light fades out at the horizon; the sky model, dim at a low sun and black below it, is strengthened to a November noon's sky light, and a shadowless sky fill tops the rest up to one floor of light from the horizon up, warm at the horizon and blue below it. That floor eases to the blue hour's at the end of civil twilight (sun at −6°), lit by the sky alone, and to the moon's across nautical twilight. Nights (sun below −12°) are moonlit: a cool moon with soft shadows and a blue sky fill keep the plant readable. The light follows the weather for the instant shown, the dashboard's now and the time slider's: the diffuse share of the weather forecast's global irradiance (ADR 17) dims the sun's own light to nearly none under a closed sky, widens its shadows, thickens the sky model's clouds and turns the fill grey, while an overcast noon stays day; without weather for the instant the sky is clear. One pure function maps elevation and diffuse share to the light; there is one directional light, one fill and no pass for any of it. Ambient occlusion runs as one pass and is off on the lite and still motion tiers (ADR 21); the low-power switch shows the 2D flow instead.
- **Strings:** a faint glow on the solar amber ramp in their cells, by share of peak.
- **Theme:** the page background, fog and exposure follow light or dark mode.
- **Rendering quality:** antialiasing is on, and tone mapping is filmic.

**Motion.** There is still no continuous loop. Short ease-in-out tweens run, then rendering stops:
- the camera flies to a preset in about 600 ms;
- tints and labels ease to each tick's values in about 400 ms.

Energy flow shows as tint and label arrows.

**Labels.** Labels are ambient-glass chips with an icon, an animated value and a flow-colour dot. They sit in rows around the house, never overlapping and inside the canvas, below the hero's headline: the strings above the house; below it the inverter and battery nearest, then grid, house and car. A row runs in its markers' order across the canvas, so its leader lines do not cross, and wraps when it is too wide; a chip without a marker sits beside its row's marked chips. A leader is a thin line from a ring at the marker to the chip's edge. The chips follow the camera only once it has moved the house or a marker by more than about 16 px, so a small move leaves them in place. Tapping a chip opens that equipment's detail sheet.

**Building it.** Anyone should get from nothing to their roof in minutes, on a phone, a tablet or with a mouse.
- **Templates.** An empty scene offers starting points: detached house with gable roof, bungalow with flat roof, house with hip roof, L-house (two blocks), terrace end, and empty. A template only fills the scene; nothing remembers it.
- **Handles.** Selecting an object shows handles in 3D:
  - arrows on its walls drag width and depth; an opening's drag its width and height;
  - a handle on the ridge or high eave drags the pitch;
  - a ring rotates it;
  - dragging its footprint moves it;
  - each point of a fence has its own handle that drags that point.
- **Snapping.** Lengths snap to 10 cm, angles to 5°, and edges to other blocks' edges and the compass axes. The live value shows beside the handle while dragging.
- **Modules.** Modules are placed on the roof in 3D, not in a separate grid. Selecting a roof face, in the inspector or by striking it, shades the roof outside its setback and over its chimneys and roof windows. A ghost module follows the pointer over the face, snapped 2 cm off the nearest module's edges and in line with it, else to 1 cm; it shows red where it would not fit. A tap places a module there; a drag across free roof lays a row along the drag, each module 2 cm past the last. A drag that starts on a module moves it, snapped like the ghost and held at the last place it fits. A tap on a module selects it: the inspector shows its string, which can be changed to none, another string of its module type or a new one, its orientation, its optimizer and its removal. "Fill face" fills the free roof row by row from the lower left in the grid's orientation; "Clear face", the module type, the orientation for new modules and the setback stay in the inspector. A chimney or roof window added or moved onto modules removes the modules it covers, in the same undo step. On a phone a module is about 10 px across from the first view, too small for a finger, so selecting a face flies the camera straight in front of it until the face fills the canvas; leaving the face returns to the view before, unless the admin has orbited since.
- **Strings.** Pick a string, or add one, then drag across modules to wire them in the order the drag crosses them. A thin line in the string's tint runs through its modules' centres from a start marker. A tap on a module of the picked string unwires it.
- **Inspector.** It shows the exact numbers. Editing one moves the handles, and every handle drag is one undo step.
- **Touch.** One finger on a handle drags it, and on empty space orbits. Two fingers pan and zoom. A handle's touch target is at least 44 px, whatever its drawn size. A gesture that starts on the selected face, or on a module while a string is picked, lays or wires; one that starts anywhere else orbits. A second finger puts a drag back and pans and zooms instead. Every drag is one undo step.
- **Layout.** The canvas takes the full height on the left. A glass inspector on the right uses the drill-in pattern: Buildings, Surroundings, Equipment, Modules, Strings. Clicking an object in 3D drills the inspector to it. The module-grid editor is its own level. On a portrait tablet the inspector is a bottom sheet that can be pulled up. On a phone the canvas fills the screen and the inspector is that bottom sheet, peeking at its header; "+" opens a sheet of everything that can be added, and an undo button sits on the canvas. The inspector rests above the shell's bottom bar (ADR 21) and nothing of it shows behind the bar.
- **Zones.** The canvas has four fixed zones and the rest is the camera's: the time slider along the top; the views' button at the top right; one status line at the lower left; undo and "+" at the lower right on a phone. Save, redo and the day the layout applies from are in the inspector's foot. The status line is a single row that never wraps or grows, and what does not fit scrolls sideways: where the powers come from, each string's power beside the colour its modules glow in, the connected cars, and a missing location. A pulled-up inspector hides the lower zones instead of covering them, so no two overlays ever overlap.

## Considered options

- **A viewer only.** Rejected: a picture on top of typed numbers adds nothing the form does not have.
- **Free-form roof modelling.** Rejected for now: parametric blocks give exact planes and work on a tablet.
- **Modules on a fixed grid per face, or rows that slide.** Rejected: real roofs carry staggered rows, mixed orientations and modules fitted round a window or chimney.
- **Shading in three.js.** Rejected: the forecast and the scene would disagree.
- **Threlte.** Rejected: a wrapper that must follow every Svelte major.
- **Map tiles to trace the roof.** Rejected: privacy, licensing and network.
- **SunReye-drawn bodies per class, picked through a car catalogue from the EVCC vehicle title.** Rejected: a stylised body looks like a toy beside the realistic house, and a catalogue is upkeep for a cosmetic. One de-badged default plus uploads covers every car.
- **The full textured car model (2.5 MB).** Rejected: about 2.5 minutes over a slow link, for detail nobody sees from the yard.
- **A numbers-only builder.** Rejected: homeowners think in roofs and drags; the numbers stay in the inspector for precision.

## Consequences

The builder ships after 5.0.0 (ADR 1). The `pv` crate models every module, which also improves the forecast of plants entered by form. The default car keeps a real car's shape, without badge or name; its CC BY credit must stay on the About page. Inverter profiles gain their maximum DC input voltage. The dashboard loads three.js when a scene exists.
