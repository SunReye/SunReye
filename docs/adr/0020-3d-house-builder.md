# 20. 3D house builder

Status: accepted (2026-11-09), amended (2026-11-10, 2026-11-12, 2026-11-13, 2026-11-14, 2026-11-15, 2026-11-17, 2026-11-19, 2026-11-20, 2026-11-22, 2026-11-23, 2026-11-24, 2026-11-25)

## Context

The PV layout (ADR 17) is entered as numbers: planes, module counts, MPPT inputs. Homeowners think in roofs. Shading, optimizers and strings that span roof faces cannot be described as one plane per string. The owner wants to build the house, place modules like a PV planner, see each string's production in 3D, and make that live scene the dashboard.

## Decision

**What it is.** An editor for the geometry of the PV layout. The layout stays the source of truth; the form stays for anyone who does not want 3D. The admin edits; viewers, and the cloud web app later, see the scene read-only.

**Scene.** The scene is part of the PV layout, owned by the layout feature module (ADR 19). Saving it is one configuration change with an effective-from date. Planes and strings are derived from it and are not edited separately once a scene exists. Units are metres, y up, origin at the first block's corner; a compass dial rotates the scene to true north. Plane azimuths follow ADR 17. No map or satellite tiles: they would send the home's address to a tile service. No map data is fetched.

- **Blocks.** A rectangular footprint (width, depth, eave height, rotation) with one roof: flat, mono-pitch, gable or hip, at a pitch. A plant has several blocks. Dormers, L-shapes and every other outline are free-form roofs (below). The compass dial's angle is the bearing of the scene's −z axis; a block's width runs along x and its depth along z from its corner, turned clockwise seen from above. A mono-pitch roof rises from the front eave (+z), a gable's ridge runs along the width, a hip's along the longer side.
  - **Overhang.** A block's roof reaches past its walls by an eave overhang (Traufe) and a verge overhang (Ortgang), in metres measured level. Its faces are the roof of the footprint widened by the overhang with the eaves lowered down the slope, so the ridge stays put: a mono-pitch or gable roof takes the eave overhang front and back and the verge overhang at the sides, a flat or hip roof the eave overhang all round. The walls stay under the roof as it meets them; carport posts too. The overhang shades and carries module grids like the rest of the face, and a face's grid and chimneys are placed from its widened outline. A block stored without one has none; the builder's new block starts with 0.5 m at the eaves and 0.3 m at the verges. A negative overhang is refused.
  - **Name** (2026-11-25). A block may carry a name, as "Garage" or "Anbau", up to 80 characters and not blank; without one an editor numbers it by its place. A name is no more than a label: nothing reads it but a list of the roofs.
  - **Colours.** A block may carry a roof colour and a wall colour, `#rrggbb`, drawn as a flat matte tint with no pattern; without one a wall is clay and a roof its grey (Look). Swatches offer common colours (roofs: Ziegelrot, Anthrazit, Schwarz, Braun; walls: Weiß, Creme, Grau) beside a custom colour. Another format is refused.
  - **Roof chimneys.** A roof chimney is an upright box on one roof face of a block, named by block and face like a module grid: its centre across and up the face's outline, its level width across the slope and depth along the fall line, and its height above the roof where its upper side meets it; its top is level. It shades like any face, and no module may overlap its footprint. One without a positive size, on no face or not wholly on its face is refused. It may be turned in its face (2026-11-25), degrees clockwise seen from above with the eave below; its footprint's corners turn about its centre, and what keeps modules off it, and it off a roof window, is the box round them. One stored without a turn has none.
  - **Roof windows.** A roof window lies flush in one roof face, named like a roof chimney: its centre across and up the face's outline, its width across the slope and its length up it. It is drawn as a framed glass pane and never shades; no module may overlap it. One without a positive size, on no face, not wholly on its face or overlapping a roof chimney or another roof window is refused. It may be turned in its face as a roof chimney is (2026-11-25).
  - **Existing PV** (2026-11-23). Modules already on a roof that stay are one rectangle on one roof face, named like a roof window: its centre across and up the face's outline, its width across the slope and its length up it. It is drawn as a dark module field and never shades; it is not part of the plant, so it produces nothing. No new module may overlap it, and a roof chimney moved, or a roof window moved or placed, keeps off it. One without a positive size, on no face, not wholly on its face or overlapping a roof chimney, a roof window or other existing PV is refused. It follows its face as a module grid does. A scene stored without any loads and saves unchanged. Adding and moving it in the builder waits.
  - **What is known of a face** (2026-11-23). Any roof face, named by block and face, may carry its covering, tiles, slate, sheet metal or bitumen, and whether its pitch is confirmed or estimated. A face that carries neither has an unknown covering and an estimated pitch. They are stored beside the faces, one entry per face at most, and follow their face as module grids do; one naming no face is refused. Both products read them; nothing in the yield does.
  - **Free-form roofs** (2026-11-19). A block may instead have a polygon footprint and a roof of free-form roof faces, each a flat polygon given by its corners in the scene's metres: drawn by hand, or taken from a surveyed building model in SunReye Plan (ADR 25). Such a face is named by block and face like any other, so module grids, roof chimneys and roof windows stand on it unchanged, and the `pv` crate derives its plane, outline and setback as it does for the four roofs. It need not be convex; the `pv` crate splits it for shading. The walls drop from the footprint and reach up to the faces above them. The block has no overhang of its own: its faces are as given. One that is not flat, has fewer than three corners, crosses itself, faces downward or lies outside every wall of its block is refused. A scene stored without one loads and saves unchanged; a core older than this refuses a scene that has one, as it refuses every field it does not know.
    - *Stored* (2026-11-23). A block's free-form part holds its footprint, corners as x and z, and its faces, corners as x, y and z: the scene's frame before the compass turns it. Its corner, size, eave and roof stay in the file and go unused; its rotation holds how far it was turned whole (2026-11-25): `turnBlock` turns its footprint, faces and points about the footprint's centroid; an overhang on it is refused. A footprint of fewer than three corners, or one that crosses itself, is refused.
    - *Flat* means no corner lies more than 2 cm from the face's plane, the plane through the corners' mean square to their Newell normal. A surveyed model's faces are flat to millimetres, and a hand-drawn face is kept flat by the builder; 2 cm lets rounding pass and refuses a face bent by a wrong height. Modules lie on that plane, and the face's corners do not shade them.
    - *Front.* A face's corners run counter-clockwise seen from above; that side is its front. Listed clockwise it faces downward, and upright it faces neither up nor down: both are refused, as is a face with a repeated corner or no area.
    - *Outside every wall:* seen from above it shares no area with the footprint. A face reaching past the walls is its own overhang.
    - *Across.* A sloped face's across runs level along it, as on the four roofs; a level face's along the scene's x, so its modules stay put while its corners are redrawn.
    - *Laying frame* (2026-11-25). A sloped face's modules lie in rows along its eave, as on any face. A level free-form face has no eave, so its rows run along its longest edge; of edges as long to a centimetre, the one nearest the scene's x axis turned by the block's rotation, so what stands on a level face turns with a block turned whole (2026-11-25). Before this a level free-form face took the scene's x axis, so a traced flat roof turned against the compass got its rows in a staircase across it; its stored modules move with the new frame.
    - *Walls.* Each side of the footprint carries a wall up to the faces above it, stepping where they step; where no face stands over it, none. An open block stands a post at each footprint corner up to the faces there. Wall openings and equipment markers name a wall of the four sides, so on a free-form block they are refused.
    - *In the builder* (2026-11-23). Each face and wall is drawn whole, convex or not; where two faces rise from a line they share, it is a valley and takes no ridge cap. The block's handles, its size, eave, rotation, roof and overhang, a snap to its edges and what a tap on its wall adds all name the rectangle, which goes unused, so it offers none of them; its colours and carport switch stay. Its lines change it (*Drawing a roof*).
  - **Drawing a roof** (2026-11-20, redone 2026-11-23). Making a roof must be as easy as clicking its lines together, and every shape must be possible.
    - *Tracing.* Seen from above, the person clicks along every roof line they see: eave, ridge, hip, valley, verge. Each click joins to the last and snaps to the points and lines already there. Every closed loop of lines is a roof face, by itself. Nothing is guessed: the builder adds no edge nobody drew and never decides which roof was meant.
    - *Pulling up.* The drawing has no heights. The house is pulled up out of the ground in two moves: the eaves to their height, then the ridges to theirs, which gives the pitches; every other point follows. The builder takes the outer boundary for eaves and the highest inner lines for ridges, and the person may change what a line is. Any point or line may then be given its own height, by pulling or by number, for a ridge that steps down, eaves at two heights or a hip that starts partway up.
      - *Eaves and ridges* (2026-11-23). A point is an eave or a ridge, the person's word first. Without one, a point that is a corner of the outline, where it turns, is an eave; any other point an inner line ends at is a ridge, so a gable's ridge ending on its verges is one, and a hip's hips, which end at corners, are not, unless that line only runs straight on through it, as where a line was clicked in two; any other point on the outline is an eave. A line is what both its points are; changing what a line is changes both. Pulling the eaves gives every eave point the height, pulling the ridges every ridge point; a point whose height was set keeps it. A point neither eave, ridge nor set follows (*Flat faces*). The roofs this guesses wrong, a Mansard roof's break, a Krüppelwalm's hips, a mono-pitch roof's high side, are put right by changing a line or setting a height.
    - *Flat faces.* A face of four or more corners is flat only for certain heights. A pull keeps faces flat by moving the points that depend on it. Heights the person set are never changed for them: a face they cannot be flat with is shown in a warning colour and carries no modules until it is.
      - *Following* (2026-11-23). Eave, ridge and set points hold their heights. A face with three of them not in one line seen from above has a plane, the one fitting them best, and its other points take their height on it; that repeats until no point changes. Each edit does it, so a line drawn on a pulled roof follows too. A point no such face reaches keeps its height.
      - *Bent* (2026-11-23). A face of a traced block whose corners are more than 2 cm off its plane is bent. It is stored as every traced face is, its corners at its points, and a traced block's bent face is not refused. A module grid on it is refused and nothing is laid or filled on it, so the edit that bends a face drops its grid; its roof chimneys, roof windows and existing PV stay. It shades as its corners say.
    - *Dormers.* A dormer is its own small roof traced on a face, and it cuts its outline out of that face. It shades what stands beside it like any part of the house. This settles how a dormer cuts its face.
      - *Traced* (2026-11-23). A dormer is a closed loop of lines traced inside a face of a traced roof, apart from that face's lines or joined to them by one line or at one point; the faces inside the loop are its faces, and the face around it is the face it stands on. A line drawn inside a traced face, seen from above, belongs to that face's block. A loop joined by one line leaves the face around it its face: the line belongs to neither.
      - *The cut* (2026-11-23). The face a dormer stands on keeps its outline as stored; the `pv` crate takes the faces of a traced block lying inside another, seen from above, as holes in that one's module area. A hole's edge is an edge of the face, so a grid's setback holds from it too, and a module reaching into one is refused as one crossing the setback. The edit that cuts a face, or moves a cut, drops that face's grid, as bending one does; its roof chimneys, roof windows and existing PV stay. The face is drawn and shades whole: under a raised dormer it lies inside it.
      - *Heights* (2026-11-23). A dormer's points are neither the house's eaves nor its ridges. While not set they lie on the plane of the face it stands on, so a dormer traced on a pulled roof lies on it and follows when the house is pulled. A dormer is pulled up as a roof is, by its own eaves and ridges, the corners of its outline and the ends of its inner lines: to a height, but never below its face. A point pulled above its face is set; one left on it follows the face. A line the person makes an eave or a ridge on a dormer is the dormer's, so only its own pulls raise it. A point splitting a line between two set points is set, at its height along the line, so a line drawn to a raised dormer leaves it standing.
      - *Walls* (2026-11-23). Each edge of a dormer's outline carries a wall from the face it stands on up to the dormer's faces: its front and its cheeks. They shade, and are drawn, as walls.
    - *Ready shapes.* The roofs and dormers common in Germany are placed with one click and then adjusted, each as the same lines a person could have traced: Satteldach, Pultdach and versetztes Pultdach, Flachdach with or without a parapet, Walmdach, Krüppelwalmdach, Zeltdach, Mansarddach and Mansardwalmdach, Schleppdach, Sheddach, Schmetterlingsdach, Tonnendach; Schleppgaube, Satteldachgaube, Walmgaube, Flachdachgaube, Zwerchhaus. Each is also a test: the tracing tool must be able to make every one of them by clicks alone.
      - *Placed* (2026-11-23). A ready roof is placed at a corner and a size, its width along x, its depth along z, its eave height and its top, a ridge, a mono-pitch roof's high side or a parapet; it is a new traced block and touches none. It is clicked as a person would, then has what the outline does not tell set as a person would: a mono-pitch roof's high side and a butterfly's valley marked, a Krüppelwalm's hips and a Mansard roof's break given their heights. A point has one height, so a step between two parts of one roof, a versetztes Pultdach's, a Sheddach's teeth, a parapet's inner wall, is a strip 10 cm wide seen from above; a Tonnendach is six strips. **(amended 2026-11-25)** A ready roof may be placed turned about its corner, clockwise seen from above, so it lands square to a house that does not face north, and `@sunreye/scene` gives the smallest rectangle around a footprint to place it on; turning one already placed is the editor's.
      - *Ready dormers* (2026-11-23). A ready dormer is placed on a face at its middle seen from above, a width across the face's slope and a depth up it, its front toward the face's low side. It is clicked as a person would and pulled up by its own eaves and ridges: its top, a ridge, a shed's high side or a flat roof, meets the face at its back, and its front eave stands at a height between the face and that top, halfway without one. A Schleppgaube is a rectangle; a Flachdachgaube the same, level at its top; a Satteldachgaube a ridge up its middle, its cheeks meeting the face where its eaves do; a Walmgaube the same with a hip at its front. A Zwerchhaus stands on the face's eave: its gable is in the house's wall, its eaves at the house's, its valleys run up the face to where its ridge meets it, and its ridge and that point are set; touching the outline, it is part of the house, not a dormer. One not wholly on its face, on a face without slope, or not fitting its size is refused.
    - *Special constructions,* all by the same tracing: L- and T-shaped houses, roofs joined at different heights, towers and bays, an attached garage or carport, a terrace cut into a roof. A curved roof is made of flat strips, since a module grid and the yield need one plane per face.
    - *One representation.* The lines stay the roof's source: moving one changes the faces, and a module grid stays on a face that still exists. A roof delivered by a building model in SunReye Plan (ADR 25) arrives as the same lines and points and is corrected with the same tool. A traced roof is stored as a free-form roof; the four block roofs stay for a house entered by numbers.
    - *Where.* In SunReye Plan the lines are drawn over the aerial image; Home draws them over the plain ground.
    - *Tracing on a surface.* What is traced on may carry heights, supplied by the builder's consumer: SunReye Plan's drone capture (Plan's ADR 12). A click then lands in three dimensions, the point takes its height from the surface, and nothing is pulled up. The tool is built for this from the start, since adding a third dimension to a click later would touch every part of it.
    - *Rejected.* Shaping a parametric block to the image by handles and numbers (SunReye Plan's prototype): the numbers stay, for corrections. Marking only eaves and ridges and letting the builder add the rest (the first version of this paragraph): it had to guess which roof was meant, and could not make every shape.
    - *Stored* (2026-11-23). A traced roof is one free-form block whose free-form part keeps its lines beside its faces: its points, each as x, y and z with y its height now; which of them had their height set, by the person or by a surface, so that no pull changes it; its lines, each a pair of points; for each face the points its corners are, in order; and the points the person made eaves and those they made ridges (*Eaves and ridges*, 2026-11-23). The faces stay stored as every free-form face is, so the `pv` crate reads a traced roof as any other and checks only that each face's corners are the points it names. A block without lines is a free-form roof entered by its faces and is changed only by them.
    - *Faces follow the lines.* Each edit derives the faces again from the lines' closed loops, seen from above. A loop that keeps three or more of an old face's points is that face: it keeps its place in the block, so its module grid, roof chimneys and roof windows stay on it, and a point added on its edge or moved leaves it the same face. Of two loops that keep the same face's points, the one keeping more of them stays it, then the larger. A loop that keeps none is a new face: it takes the place of a face that went, the first first, else a place after the others. A face whose loop is gone takes its grid, chimneys and windows with it; only where more faces went than came do the faces after them move up a place, with what stands on them, as the blocks after a removed block do. So moving a line, pulling up and a dormer leave every unchanged face its place. Each edit answers where each of the block's old faces went. A point no line uses is dropped.
    - *Crossings.* A line crossing another where no click was splits both there: a point is added, never a line. Two lines on top of each other are one.
    - *One roof, one block.* A line that neither ends on a traced block, crosses one nor lies inside one of its faces starts a new block; a line that would join two traced blocks is refused.
      - *Roofs at different heights* (2026-11-25). A house and the garage against its wall, or a stepped house, are roofs of their own at their own heights. The tracing names the roof its lines go into: a new one, where the first line starts a new block even where it ends on, crosses or runs along another roof's lines, and the chain's later lines join that block; or a roof already traced. A line so named is split only by its own block's lines, and a point of another roof it ends on gives it its place, not its block. Each block keeps its own eaves, ridges and walls: a block's walls rise to its own faces, so where a lower roof meets a higher one the higher block's wall shows above the lower roof as the step. The add edit carries the roof it named, so a replay makes the same blocks. Without one, the rule above holds.
      - *Points* (2026-11-25). What is traced is edited where it lies, with no chain being drawn: a traced point dragged moves level to where it snaps, keeping its height; a double click on a traced line puts a point into it there, at the height between its ends; Del on a point tapped, before a line is drawn from it, takes it out, the two lines meeting there joined into one (more or fewer take theirs with it). A traced line dragged moves the straight run it lies in, on through each point only two lines meet at while they keep straight: a side split by a point clicked on it, or where another roof meets it, moves whole. Each is one edit (`movePoint`, `split`, `removePoint`, `move`), refused where lines would cross; the faces follow. A traced point or line under the pointer is grabbed.
      - *Outlines left open* (2026-11-25). A block traced with no face is no roof. A chain traced into a new roof that ends unclosed (Esc, or a click on its last point) takes its block out; a chain started anywhere first takes out every block left open before, but the one it starts on, which it goes on with. Each is one edit (`removeBlock`), so Ctrl+Z brings the outline back. While tracing into a roof named by its number, nothing is taken out, as that would renumber it.
    - *Footprint.* While a traced block's faces make no single outline seen from above, as before the first loop closes, it has no footprint and so no walls, and its faces need not stand over one. Otherwise its footprint is that outline.
    - *Heights.* A click on plain ground lands at height 0 and leaves the point's height unset; a click on a surface that carries heights sets it. Where set heights bend a loop, its face keeps its place and is drawn in the warning colour until it is flat (*Flat faces*), stored as *Bent* says. A face is flat to the free-form roofs' tolerance, 2 cm.
    - *The operations* (2026-11-23). Adding, moving and removing a line, with its snaps, and deriving the faces are pure: `@sunreye/scene` takes a scene and returns the next one, with no browser and no 3D library, so a consumer may run an edit where it keeps the scene. Pulling up, a point's height, ready shapes and dormers join them there. The builder's canvas has a tracing mode beside laying and wiring, drawn from above over what it is given, the aerial image included: each click snaps, names its snap and shows the length of the line being drawn, and the canvas hands the next scene to its consumer, who keeps it. Pulling the eaves, pulling the ridges, changing what a line is and setting a point's height joined them (2026-11-23). Once points have heights, a click picks a point or a line by its pixels on the screen, as a raised point stands over ground other than its own.
      - *Every edit named* (2026-11-23). Each edit of a traced roof is the same named edit with the arguments of its operation, whether the canvas makes it, handed to the consumer with the next scene, or the builder's tracing bar does, handed to the frame's consumer: adding a line with the reach its clicks snapped within, moving or removing one, pulling the eaves or ridges, marking a line, setting a point's height, placing a ready roof or dormer, pulling a dormer. `@sunreye/scene` replays one on a scene, so a consumer keeping the scene elsewhere makes the same next scene. A ready shape is picked from the tracing bar's menu and placed by the next click: a roof centred on it, a dormer on the face under it.
  - **Racks** (2026-11-19). A rack field tilts its modules off what they stand on: rows on a flat roof face, or on level ground inside a footprint like a block's. It has a tilt, an azimuth, a row spacing and one module type, its rows portrait or landscape, single or back to back (east–west). A module's plane is its rack's, not its face's (ADR 17), and it is wired and may carry an optimizer like any other. Rows shade each other and are shaded as every module is; the frame under them casts no shade. A rack field on a pitched face, with rows that touch, or reaching past its face's setback or its footprint is refused. A scene stored without one loads and saves unchanged; a core older than this refuses a scene that has one. Sloping ground, ballast and wind load are not modelled. Open before build: the largest field the per-module model and the browser carry.
- **Modules.** The admin defines the roofs first, then places modules freely on each roof face, like a PV planner. Each module has its own place and its own orientation, portrait or landscape; each face has an edge setback. Modules are wired into strings, which may span faces. Optimizers are set per module.
  - A face's module grid holds one module type. A module's place is its lower left corner across and up the face's outline, in metres, seen from the front; its footprint is the module type's size, its long side across the slope in landscape. A flat roof's across runs along the block's width. The grid's orientation is the one a new module takes.
  - No two modules may overlap, and none may cross the setback or overlap a roof chimney, roof window or existing PV; the builder keeps them 2 cm apart.
  - A scene stored with a grid's rows and columns loads with each module at its cell's corner, in the grid's orientation, so nothing moves; it is saved with places.
  - The scene is stored with its layout version as one document in the `pv` crate's schema, in which a module names its string by id. Through the API a module names its string by position in the version's strings, so new strings need no id first.
  - The core derives each string's module count, optimizer count and plane, the plane of its first module, with the `pv` crate; the browser's numbers are not trusted. A string wired through a grid of another module type is refused.
  - A wired module keeps its place along its string's wiring. The string runs through its modules in that order; a module without a place follows them. A module wired into a string takes the place after the string's last; unwired, it loses its place.
- **Obstacles.** Opaque boxes (a neighbour, a wall), cylinders (a pole), trees (a trunk with a crown, broadleaf or conifer) and hedges (a straight row), each with position, size and height. A tree shades all year; leaf fall is not modelled.
  - A broadleaf's crown is a sphere whose top is the tree's height; a conifer's is a cone of the crown's radius from a fifth of the tree's height to its top. A trunk a tenth of the crown's radius carries either. A tree stored without a kind is broadleaf.
  - A hedge has a corner, a length along x, a depth along z, a height and a rotation, like a block's footprint, and shades as a box. It draws as a plain body in the crowns' tone.
- **Garage.** A block with walls and any roof; it needs no kind of its own.
- **Surroundings.** Ground surfaces and fences are drawn and never shade; the `pv` crate stores them and ignores them for shading.
  - A *ground surface* is a rectangle on the plot with a footprint like a block's and one finish: lawn, paving, driveway, gravel or path. Later surfaces draw over earlier ones. Each is a flat grey a step apart from the studio floor and from the others; without any, the plot is the floor (Look).
  - A *fence* is a polyline on the ground with a height and one finish: wood, metal or wall.
  - The `pv` crate places both on the ground for the browser to draw; a surface without a positive width and depth, or a fence with fewer than two points or no positive height, is refused. A fence is drawn in a flat clay tone of its finish.
- **Equipment.** Markers for inverters and batteries, each linked to its device and placed on a block's wall, inside or outside.
  - A marker names its device, its kind (inverter or battery, so a hybrid inverter may carry one of each), its block, the wall (front along +z, right along +x, back, left) and its distance from the wall's left end seen from outside. It hangs at 1.5 m, at most half the eave, 10 cm off the wall; its label's anchor floats 1 m above the block's highest point. The `pv` crate places it, so the browser and the core agree; a marker past its wall's ends or on no block is refused. A marker of a removed device shows nothing.
- **Openings.** Windows and doors in the blocks' walls. They are drawn and never shade; the `pv` crate stores them, fits them on their walls for the browser, and ignores them for shading.
  - An opening names its kind (window, French window, front door or garage door), its block, the wall like a marker, and the distance from the wall's left end to its own left edge seen from outside; it has a width, a height and a sill height, 0 when left out. One on no block or on a carport, without a positive size, past its wall's ends, reaching above the eave, a door off the ground, or sharing wall with an earlier opening is refused. A scene stored without openings loads unchanged.
  - *Look:* plain shapes, no maps: a clay frame of 7 cm bars 5 cm proud of the wall, with dark glass or a leaf lying inset in it. A window is two panes that mirror the sky, either side of a mullion, on a sill; a front door a grey leaf under a glazed fanlight and a small canopy; a garage door a grey leaf. The wall behind is not cut.
  - *Night:* the front door's fanlight and about three windows in five glow warm, each window chosen by where it sits, so the same ones glow on every draw and the house looks lived in. They come on across dusk with the night's light (Look). The glow is the glass's own emissive colour, and the light on the surface below, the floor or a lower roof under the window, is one decal for all of them: no light source is added. Nothing animates it, so the still tier shows it as it is.
  - *Night light per opening* (2026-11-19): an opening with glass, a window, a French window or a front door, carries a `light`: `auto`, the rule above and what an opening stored without one has; `on`, lit every night; or `off`, never lit. German UI: Automatisch, Leuchtet, Dunkel. A garage door has no glass and no choice. `auto` is left out of the stored scene, so a scene stored before loads, lights and saves unchanged and needs no migration; a core older than this refuses a scene that names a `light`, as it refuses every field it does not know.

- **From the consumer** (2026-11-23). The builder takes an aerial image and neighbours from whoever embeds it, and fetches neither. Home supplies none, and its builder looks and behaves as without them. Neither is saved with the scene.
  - *Frame.* Both are given in metres east and north of the scene's origin, north up as a map is, whatever the compass dial says: the frame the `pv` crate shades in.

**Editing.** Undo and redo until save. Saving checks that modules sit inside their face's setback and overlap neither each other nor a roof chimney, roof window or existing PV. Unassigned modules are shown and produce nothing. A string whose open-circuit voltage at −10 °C exceeds the inverter's maximum DC input voltage (ADR 5) raises a warning, never a refusal, because the plant may already exist. That voltage scales the datasheet's Voc by its voltage coefficient, as string sizing does. The layout's answer carries the warnings, for strings entered by form too. The browser runs the same checks through the `pv` crate before saving.

- **MPPT window warnings** (2026-11-19). While editing, the builder also warns, and never refuses, where the layout leaves an MPPT input's limits (ADR 5), as far as the inverter's profile gives them (ADR 9):
  - a string whose open-circuit voltage at −10 °C stays below the input's start voltage: it never starts;
  - a string whose maximum-power voltage at a cell temperature of 70 °C lies below the input's voltage window: it is held off its peak on hot days;
  - a string whose maximum-power voltage at −10 °C lies above the window: it is clipped on cold days;
  - an input whose parallel strings' short-circuit currents sum to more than its maximum input current;
  - an input whose parallel strings differ in module count (ADR 17's lint);
  - a string on an input the inverter does not have.
  Voltages scale by the datasheet's voltage coefficient from 25 °C, as the cold voltage does. Each warning is one line beside its string or input in the inspector and names the figure and the limit. The `pv` crate reckons them in the browser; the layout's answer keeps carrying the cold-voltage warning only, until another client needs the rest. The crate's warnings also carry a string whose open-circuit voltage at −10 °C exceeds its inverter's maximum DC input voltage, each inverter's maximum read with its inputs, so a second consumer (ADR 25) checks a layout in one call; the builder keeps that warning apart, as above (2026-11-24).

**Physics.** Shading is computed in the `pv` crate (ADR 17), per bypass-diode substring, against blocks with their overhangs, roof chimneys, obstacles and carport roofs; diffuse light by each substring's sky view, where a run lays one (ADR 17). The `pv` crate also turns blocks, roofs and obstacles into the faces and solids the browser draws, so what is drawn is what shades: the forecast shades with each face as a fan of triangles and with the solids. The core's forecast and the browser run the same code, so the scene and the forecast never disagree, but for the scene's diffuse shade, which the forecast does not yet lay (ADR 17). three.js only draws. Editing asks the crate too: it keeps a changed opening on its wall, finds a new one's place, keeps a dragged chimney on its face, finds the chimney a click strikes, and lays out the walls and chimney footings the handles stand on. The browser holds no copy of that geometry.

**Rendering.** three.js directly, without a Svelte wrapper. The scene is built by pure functions from the layout and drawn by one thin component. It needs WebGL2. It redraws only on a live tick or a camera move, never in a continuous loop. All placed modules are one instanced draw, each with its own tint. The sun's shadow map is drawn again only when the sun, the 3D quality or what casts a shadow changes: the built scene, the plot it is fitted to, or the parked cars. The sky that lights the scene is made again only once the sun has moved far enough to show: 5° under a sun at least 35° up, 2.5° below that, 1° below 15° where the sky turns quickly, and not at all once the sun is 10° under the horizon. Measured, a sky that stale moves 99 pixels in 100 by under 2 % of their range. An orbit camera offers preset views (north, east, south, west, top, isometric) from one button on the canvas, whose menu is closed until asked for; each viewer's last view is kept in their browser. The `pv` crate reaches the browser through its own WASM glue crate (ADR 2). The admin edits at every width, the phone included: setup's layout step is the builder, and a homeowner may set up from a phone.

**Loading.** The dashboard never waits for the scene. three.js and the scene load after the dashboard is interactive: its two generated maps in a worker, the plant built in slices, shader programs compiled ahead, and the first frame drawn hidden. Meanwhile the hero shows the scene's own studio backdrop with a faint ground shadow; "3D-Ansicht lädt …" appears only after a second. Once the GPU has finished the first frame, the scene and its labels fade in over about 350 ms; the car loads after the house and fades in on its own. With 2D chosen, under the low-power switch or without WebGL2, three.js is never requested; a first switch from 2D keeps the flow until the scene can fade over it. A failed load leaves the 2D flow with "Erneut versuchen".

**Time slider.** A date and time slider moves the sun and the shadows. Per-string production comes from measured values or estimates for past dates, the forecast for today and the next two days, and a clear-sky run of the `pv` crate in the browser for any other date. Once readings exist, each MPPT input's learned correction and its residual over the last 30 days are shown in the inspector's Strings group, beside the strings they judge, so a misplaced face or a missing obstacle is found there and seen where it is drawn. The day is picked in a calendar and the time scrubs on a slider (ADR 21); both sit on one line. Nothing changes the scene automatically.

- A day with nothing stored, such as one before the first reading, runs the clear sky too. The browser's clear sky caps no MPPT input, as it reads no inverter profile. The location is the admin's (ADR 9), so for a viewer the sun stays put and no clear sky runs.
- `GET /api/v1/corrections`, read like history, returns each MPPT input's learned factors and its residual: measured against modelled energy in kWh over the whole hours of the last 30 days that are both modelled on archive weather and measured, short of the AC limit, the model uncorrected. The residual is the one the correction learner last stored (2026-11-20, ADR 24): modelling 30 days per read took seconds at 0.5 CPU on the ten-year file, past the 100 ms a read may take. The learner stores it in `correction_recent` on each run, whether or not a day was learned, and again at once after each layout change, as an edit with a past effective-from date changes the modelled days the builder judges it by. Between refreshes, the window does not slide and archive weather the backfill stores (ADR 23) is not counted; both catch up at the next run, within 12 hours. Without a location it is absent.
- The clear sky is Ineichen and Perez's at sea level under a Linke turbidity of 3, with still air at 20 °C: a plant stores no altitude, and a turbidity climatology is a 30 MB table. Golden values come from pvlib.

**Live dashboard.** When a scene exists, the dashboard opens on it, updated by live ticks (ADR 9): each string's power, tinted by its share of its own peak and labelled at its centre (estimates carry "≈"); each inverter's AC power; the battery's power, direction and SOC; the car's charging power and the vehicle's SOC when EVCC reports them; grid import or export and house consumption from the `plant` device. Equipment labels point at their markers with leader lines, readable through walls; without markers they have no line. The dashboard's camera is locked until its lock toggle is opened: a finger on the scene scrolls the page and taps chips, and neither a drag nor the wheel turns or zooms it. Leaving the dashboard locks it again; the builder's camera is never locked. The 2D power flow serves when there is no scene, no WebGL2 or the low-power switch is on, and a toggle switches between them per browser. An inverter's label shows its `inverter.power`, the AC output, and its `pv.power` only when the profile maps no `inverter.power`; the label and the inverter's detail sheet show the same figure. The scene's tints and labels come from the live values only; three.js loads when the scene is shown.

**Look.** A white-box model in a studio (2026-11-16, replacing the generated finishes): like an architect's massing model, plain and matte, its realism the light's alone. It matches the SPA (ADR 21): the floor fades into the page background, with no visible horizon.
- **Materials:** matte clay in a few flat tones, with no map and no pattern. Walls, chimneys, obstacles, posts and frames are off-white; roof faces a cooler light grey, so a roof reads against its walls; a block's own roof or wall colour is a flat tint. Crowns and hedges are clay with a breath of green, trunks and wooden fences with a breath of wood, metal fences and door leaves a darker grey. Only glass reflects: the modules and the panes. The modules, the string glow and the labels are the only strong colour. No texture files ship, and only two maps are generated: a module's cells and the light a lit opening spills.

  - *Backdrop (2026-11-25):* the page colour, flat, as it lands after tone mapping. A vignette's lighter middle and darker rim read as a white glow behind the house and a ring where the floor fades.
  - *Trees:* a broadleaf's crown is one ball and a conifer's one cone, as the crate shades them, on a tapered trunk. No leaves are drawn.

  - *Nothing hidden shows.* The crate promises no winding and lays a wall's top edge in its roof's plane, so the drawing makes up for both: every pass draws both sides of a face with the normal of the side seen, the ambient-occlusion pass's own depths and normals included; a shadow is looked up on the side seen; walls lie a hair back in depth, so none shows through its roof as a line; and a pole is drawn its radius short, so a carport's post ends under its roof.

- **Modules:** full black: black cells, backsheet and frames under dark glass that mirrors the sky. Its specular strength is 0.2, so the sun and the moon leave no glare, and the sky model mirrors in it 7.5 times as strongly as it lights the clay; a pane mirrors it 3 times as strongly. A module is a body 3.5 cm proud of its roof, its satin frame round the cells.
- **Light:** the sun at its real position for the plant and time, casting soft shadows, with a sky model generated in code as the environment: image-based light, no file. The builder's time slider moves it. Light never falls as the sun rises, and a sun on the horizon already reads as day: daylight there is the sky's, not the sun's. The sun's own light fades out at the horizon; the sky model, dim at a low sun and black below it, is strengthened to one sky light at every height, and a shadowless fill tops the rest up to one floor of light from the horizon up, warm at the horizon and blue below it. That floor eases to the blue hour's at the end of civil twilight (sun at −6°), lit by the sky alone, and to the moon's across nautical twilight. Nights (sun below −12°) are moonlit: a pale moon with soft shadows and a grey-blue fill keep the plant readable. The light follows the weather for the instant shown, the dashboard's now and the time slider's: the measured clear-sky index where it has a value, else the diffuse share of the weather forecast's global irradiance (ADR 17, ADR 23) dims the sun's own light to about a third under a closed sky, fades its cast shadows out (ADR 23, 2026-11-17, replacing widened shadows), thickens the sky model's clouds and turns the fill grey, while an overcast noon stays day; without weather for the instant the sky is clear. One pure function maps elevation and diffuse share to the light; there is one directional light, one fill and no pass for any of it. Ambient occlusion runs as one pass, 2 m wide and strong, so a wall sits on the floor and an eave on its wall; it is off on the lite and still motion tiers (ADR 21); the low-power switch shows the 2D flow instead.
  - *Balance (2026-11-16):* on a white model the light is all the colour and all the form there is. A high sun's own light, warm, is stronger than the sky's and the fill's together, so a face turned to it stands clear of one turned away; a low sun's is at least two fifths of the whole. The sky model is blue and would light white faces blue, so it gives about a quarter of a shaded face's light and the mirror image in the glass; the neutral fill, a little cool from above and warm from the floor, gives the rest. A closed sky leaves a third of the sun as a soft key light: a grey day still has form.
- **Strings:** a faint glow on the solar amber ramp in their cells, by share of peak.
- **Theme:** the page background, fog and exposure follow light or dark mode; the model and its floor are the same in both, lit lower in the dark one.
- **Rendering quality:** antialiasing is on: the scene is drawn into a multisampled target, and the canvas itself has none, as only the finished picture is copied onto it. Tone mapping is Khronos PBR Neutral, exposed so that white clay in full sun stays under the page's white and its shade lies well apart: it leaves the clay's tones as they are below its shoulder and compresses above it without ever clipping. The backdrop and the fog are its exact inverse of the page colour. A per-browser *3D quality* under Settings › Display chooses Auto, Niedrig, Mittel, Hoch or Ultra; it governs only how sharply the scene is drawn, while the motion tiers (ADR 21) keep governing what moves. Tones, reflections and exposure are the same at every level: the levels differ only in the shadow map's size, the shadow's softness, the pixel ratio, the antialiasing burst and the occlusion's resolution and samples.
  - *Niedrig:* what the lite tier drew: no ambient occlusion, the pixel ratio at 1, a 1024 shadow map.
  - *Mittel:* a 2048 shadow map, the pixel ratio up to 1.5, and ambient occlusion at half that, up to 0.75 device pixels per CSS pixel, from 8 samples. Occlusion is wide and soft, and was the largest single cost of a frame on a phone.
  - *Hoch:* also contact-hardening shadows from a 4096 map, sharp where they touch their caster and softer with distance; the pixel ratio up to 2; ambient occlusion at up to 1.5 device pixels per CSS pixel, from 12 samples.
  - *Ultra:* also temporal antialiasing, ambient occlusion at full resolution, and the pixel ratio of the device.
  - *Auto* starts at Hoch where WebGL2 reports a discrete or capable GPU and the device is not a touch device, and at Mittel elsewhere. A touch device, one whose primary pointer is coarse, is a phone or a tablet: its screen has more pixels than its GPU fills at Hoch without heat, and its GPU's name does not tell it apart ("Apple GPU" is an iPhone and a desktop Mac alike). Hoch stays a choice there. Auto steps down a level each time the frame watch measures strain, remembered per browser like the tier and for as long (ADR 21: seven days). A chosen level stays where it is. The lite and still tiers cap Auto at Mittel. Nothing ships as a texture file at any level.
    - A strain counts against the drawing only while a scene is on show. Above Mittel it takes a level off the drawing and leaves the motion tier; at Mittel the tier drops to lite first, and only a strain on lite takes Auto to Niedrig.
    - The lite and still tiers drop ambient occlusion at every level, chosen or Auto, as before the setting (ADR 21). A chosen level keeps its sharpness on any tier.
    - Ultra's temporal antialiasing is a burst of 16 frames after the picture settles, each begun once the GPU has drawn the last; a frame over 50 ms ends the burst, and any change starts it over.
  - 2026-11-16: the levels had also changed the look: per-finish variation from Mittel up (per-tile hue, streaks, moss, patches, clover, wear), and in Hoch stronger relief, stronger reflections and bloom, in Ultra grass blades. The owner judged the scene worse than before, in its overall look and colours, so all of it is removed and the look before the setting is every level's.
  - 2026-11-16, later: the owner judged that look too, the generated finishes at every level, as "trying too much to make it look real with our pseudo real textures", and flat. **Camera effects (2026-11-22).** Settings › Display has separate *Tilt-Shift* and *Depth of field* switches, kept per browser and off by default. Tilt-shift keeps a band around the plant's projected centre sharp and softens above and below it. Depth of field keeps a slice at the plant's centre distance in focus. Nearer and farther surfaces, including rain and snow, soften into round bokeh as their distance from the slice grows. Both effects share one pass before tone mapping and lens glass. They apply to the dashboard and the builder's preview, with a sharp view while editing. Labels and picking are outside the pass.

**Motion.** There is no continuous loop but moving weather's (ADR 23): on the full tier, while rain, snow or wet glass move, the scene renders at the display's rate, paused while hidden. Otherwise short ease-in-out tweens run, then rendering stops:
- the camera flies to a preset in about 600 ms;
- tints and labels ease to each tick's values in about 400 ms. A tick that moves no module's glow by a hundredth of its range is drawn in one frame, and one that moves none draws nothing.

Every frame of the scene, the loop's, a flight's, an orbit's or a tint's, is held to the browser's frame limit (ADR 21).

Energy flow shows as tint and label arrows.

**Building it.** Anyone should get from nothing to their roof in minutes, on a phone, a tablet or with a mouse.
- **Templates.** An empty scene offers starting points: detached house with gable roof, bungalow with flat roof, house with hip roof, L-house (two blocks), terrace end, and empty. A template only fills the scene; nothing remembers it.
- **Handles.** Selecting an object shows handles in 3D:
  - arrows at its walls' feet drag width and depth, off the walls' faces (2026-11-20: at mid-wall they took the tap and the drag meant for the wall); an opening's drag its width and height;
  - a handle on the ridge or high eave drags the pitch;

  - dragging its footprint moves it; a block is moved by a knob on the ground before its front wall instead (2026-11-20, below);

- **Shallow views** (2026-11-19). A drag on the ground follows the pointer in a level plane. Seen from under 15° above that plane, as from the north, east, south and west views, the plane is nearly edge-on and a finger's width up the screen was tens of metres; there, the pointer's travel up and down the screen moves the object away from and toward the camera by the same metres that travel covers across the screen at the grab's distance.
- **Mistaken drags** (2026-11-20). A picked house was turned and moved by drags meant to orbit. Two grabs did it, each reproduced by a test: the ring lay on the ground round the house as soon as it was picked, a band a finger wide where an orbit starts, and turned at the first pixel; and a press anywhere on the picked block's own surface moved it after 5 px, on a phone where the house fills the canvas. A threshold alone cures neither, for an orbit travels further than any threshold. What a press does, by what it lands on:

  | The press lands on | A tap | A drag |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | open ground or sky | nothing | orbits |
  | an object that is not picked | picks it | orbits |
  | a handle of the picked object | nothing | drags it, once the pointer has gone 8 px |
  | the picked object itself, any but a block | nothing | moves it, once the pointer has gone 8 px |
  | the picked block itself | picks the wall spot, or the roof face, under it | orbits |
  | anywhere, in the turn mode | nothing | turns the picked object about its centre, once the pointer has gone 8 px: on the ring by the angle swept round it, elsewhere by the travel sideways |

  - *The turn mode.* The ring shows only after "Drehen" in the context toolbar, for whatever has one: a block, a box, a ground surface, a car place. In it the ring is the only handle drawn. A drag on the ring turns the object by the angle the pointer sweeps round its centre. Round a house that fills the canvas the ring lies outside it, so a one-finger drag anywhere else is a dial: half a degree for each pixel sideways, and nothing before the pointer has gone 8 px sideways, so a swipe up or down neither turns nor re-snaps an angle typed in the inspector. What is under the pointer follows it: where the press meets the ground before the object's middle, to the right is against the clock, and beyond the middle, as at the top of a view from above, with it. Two fingers still pan and zoom; to orbit, leave the mode. The toolbar reads the angle and offers "Fertig"; that, or picking anything else in the inspector, leaves it. Turning is rare and its mistake costly, since a house's turn is its roofs' azimuth, so it pays one tap; the angle's own row in the inspector stays for an exact figure.
  - *A block's knob.* A block fills a phone's canvas, so a drag that starts on it is far more often an orbit than a move: its surface no longer moves it, and a knob before its front wall does, by the wall's foot and beside its arrow, in sight on a phone. Every smaller object is still moved by its own surface, where a drag is nearly always meant for it. A traced block has its knob too (2026-11-26), before its southernmost corner under its centroid.
  - *A press on the object itself* (2026-11-26). Out of the turn mode, a press on the picked object's own surface moves it, though a ring's or an arrow's fingertip reach lies over it on screen: zoomed out, a small chimney's ring covered it, and a press meant to move it turned it. Only the ridge's knob and a fence's points sit on their object, and win there.
  - *Hidden handles.* A handle is drawn into the scene's depth and grabbed only where the scene does not stand before it under the pointer, a fingertip's reach allowed: seen from behind, the knob and the arrows before the front wall are neither seen through the house nor caught by a drag across its back wall. Before, every handle was drawn and grabbed over everything.

  - *An editor's ring* (2026-11-25). An editor may offer the ring beside the other handles, outside the turn mode. Its turn settles within 3° of the compass every 45° and of other blocks' walls, else on whole degrees; Shift turns free to a tenth.   - *A roof object's arrows* (2026-11-26). A roof chimney and a roof window carry an arrow at the middle of their right edge for their width and one at their upper edge for a chimney's depth or a window's length, along their own turned sides in their roof face's plane. Locking while modules are laid or wired: nothing to build, for a block has handles only on its own level, and a test holds that. Snapping to 15°: above.

- **Navigation** (2026-11-25). The canvas's consumer picks how the pointer moves the camera. `orbit` is the builder's own, for a finger: a drag orbits, the right button pans, the wheel zooms. An editor on a desk, where a mouse and a keyboard are at hand, takes `plan` or `model`, as drawing and CAD tools do (Figma, SketchUp, Onshape): a drag from empty ground pans, the middle button pans, Space held pans whatever a press lands on, and the wheel and a pinch zoom to the cursor. `plan` is seen from above and held north up: every button pans and nothing turns. It is drawn in parallel projection, as a plan on paper is: a wall never leans out toward the edge, and a roof's outline lies over its footprint wherever it is on screen. Its frustum shows at the target what the perspective camera standing there would, so a view of it keeps its meaning, and the controls' zoom becomes its distance. `model` orbits with the right button or Shift and the left; one finger orbits and two pan and pinch. Since a right drag orbits, the right button asks for a context menu only when it is let go where it was pressed, never on its press; a finger held still for half a second asks too. The canvas answers with what the press struck and its roof face; the consumer brings the menu, the kit's context menu. In an editor's navigation the face a press would pick is outlined, the cursor shows whether a press moves, picks or pans, and a press on a picked block's own surface, its roof included, moves it, as every smaller object's does: there a drag on open ground pans, so the mistaken drags above cannot arise. A free-form block moves whole, its traced points with it; what stands on a block rides along. Moving and removing a block, with what stands on it and every later block's index, is `@sunreye/scene`'s, one copy for every consumer; the last block may go.
- **Modules.** Modules are placed on the roof in 3D, not in a separate grid. Selecting a roof face, in the inspector or by striking it, shades the roof outside its setback and over its chimneys and roof windows. A ghost module follows the pointer over the face, snapped 2 cm off the nearest module's edges and in line with it, else to 1 cm; it shows red where it would not fit. A tap places a module there; a drag across free roof lays a row along the drag, each module 2 cm past the last. A drag that starts on a module moves it, snapped like the ghost and held at the last place it fits. A tap on a module selects it: the inspector shows its string, which can be changed to none, another string of its module type or a new one, its orientation, its optimizer and its removal. "Fill face" fills the free roof row by row from the lower left in the grid's orientation; fill rules may change that (2026-11-23): the gap between modules, never under 2 cm; rows and columns centred in the setback, the spare roof shared out on both sides; and a keep-out margin round each roof chimney, roof window, existing PV and dormer. The builder's own fill is the default: 2 cm, from the lower left, no margin. A face traced from a building model is a few millimetres off square, so a first column or row on the setback could cross it all along and lay nothing (2026-11-25): one that holds no module moves a centimetre in, five times at most, and the moved fill is kept only where it lays more; the setback is never crossed. A consumer of the builder that lets an installer choose them passes them; they are a call's, never the scene's. "Clear face", the module type, the orientation for new modules and the setback stay in the inspector. A chimney or roof window added or moved onto modules removes the modules it covers, in the same undo step. On a phone a module is about 10 px across from the first view, too small for a finger, so selecting a face flies the camera straight in front of it until the face fills the canvas; leaving the face returns to the view before, unless the admin has orbited since.
- **Strings: wiring mode** (2026-11-19, replacing a drag that wired at once into a picked string: nothing said the drag was armed, a drag begun on a module of the string unwired instead, and a phone showed the modules too small to hit). The inspector's Strings group and every string's level are the wiring mode.
  - *What shows.* Every string's modules glow in its colour at full strength and every string's wiring order is drawn: a thin line in its tint through its modules' centres from a start marker. Modules in no string stay grey. On a phone the camera flies onto a roof face as for laying modules: on entering, the face with the most modules in no string, and after that the face a tap on its roof names; a tap on a roof never leaves the mode.

A selection is outlined, and a line runs through it in its order from a start marker. It is held by the page, never stored, and no undo step. A press anywhere else orbits, as everywhere.
  - *Wiring a selection.* While modules are selected, the context toolbar (below) says how many and offers: wire them into the picked string, or the string chosen from its menu; into a new string of their grid's module type; unwire them; or drop the selection. Wired, they take the places after the string's last in the selection's order, one undo step. A selection that holds a grid of another module type than the string is refused with the reason in the toolbar, and nothing is wired.
  - *Auto-stringing.* The Strings group and a roof face's context toolbar offer "automatisch verdrahten" for a roof face that holds modules in no string. It runs through them in a serpentine, row by row from the lower left, and cuts the run into new strings no longer than keeps each string's open-circuit voltage at −10 °C within the inverter's maximum DC input voltage and its maximum-power voltage there within its input's window; without the inverter's limits, into one string. The strings go to the MPPT inputs that carry no string, in order, of the first inverter that lists its inputs, and a proposal never puts strings of unequal length in parallel (2026-11-19, later: the first cut did, and tripped its own warning):
    - as few strings as hold the modules, one on each free input, their module counts at most one apart, or as near as each input's own limits allow: every free input is sized by its own window and current (2026-11-19, later: the first input's stood for all);

Picking modules, an undo or a redo, or leaving the roof face and the wiring mode drops it; so does any other edit made while it shows, and that edit is not made, because it was made of the proposal (2026-11-19, later: such an edit recorded the proposal with it). Saving saves what is shown, the proposal with it. It never changes a module that is already wired.

## Considered options

- **Ground-mounted parks.** Rejected: terrain, central inverters, transformers and thousands of modules are another product for another customer, and none of it reaches a home (ADR 25). Racks cover flat roofs and small ground fields.
- **Fitting surveyed roofs to the four roof kinds.** Rejected: it discards the survey's precision, and installers plan L-shaped houses every day.

- **A viewer only.** Rejected: a picture on top of typed numbers adds nothing the form does not have.
- **Free-form roof modelling.** Rejected for now: parametric blocks give exact planes and work on a tablet.
- **Modules on a fixed grid per face, or rows that slide.** Rejected: real roofs carry staggered rows, mixed orientations and modules fitted round a window or chimney.
- **Shading in three.js.** Rejected: the forecast and the scene would disagree.
- **Threlte.** Rejected: a wrapper that must follow every Svelte major.
- **Map tiles to trace the roof.** Rejected: privacy, licensing and network. This holds for Home. SunReye Plan drapes the Land's aerial image on the ground and the roof faces and takes roofs, neighbours and trees from the Länder's models (Plan's ADR 5); the builder package therefore accepts an image and geometry from its consumer and fetches none itself (2026-11-23).
- **SunReye-drawn bodies per class, picked through a car catalogue from the EVCC vehicle title.** Rejected: a stylised body looks like a toy beside the realistic house, and a catalogue is upkeep for a cosmetic. One de-badged default plus uploads covers every car.
- **The full textured car model (2.5 MB).** Rejected: about 2.5 minutes over a slow link, for detail nobody sees from the yard.
- **Wiring by a drag into the picked string** (until 2026-11-19). Rejected after use: see Strings.
- **A free lasso around modules.** Waits: the modules a drag crosses already carry the wiring order, which a lasso would have to guess.
- **Auto-stringing the whole plant at once.** Waits in the builder: a face at a time keeps the result small enough to judge in the preview. The crate offers it to a consumer without a preview (2026-11-24).
- **Refusing a layout outside an MPPT window.** Rejected: the plant may already exist, as with the cold voltage.
- **A 3D quality step of its own for phones.** Rejected: it would differ from Mittel only in its occlusion, for a sixth choice in the setting. Mittel takes the lighter occlusion instead, and Auto starts touch devices there.
- **Hoch on every capable GPU** (until 2026-11-19). Rejected after measuring a phone: a 4096 shadow map, contact shadows and occlusion at 1.5 on a screen of pixel ratio 3 cost most of each frame.
- **Stepping tints to each tick.** Rejected: a cloud's edge moves a string's glow far enough for a step to show. Only a tick that moves nothing visibly steps.
- **A numbers-only builder.** Rejected: homeowners think in roofs and drags; the numbers stay in the inspector for precision.

## Consequences

The builder ships after 5.0.0 (ADR 1). The `pv` crate models every module, which also improves the forecast of plants entered by form. The default car keeps a real car's shape, without badge or name; its CC BY credit must stay on the About page. Inverter profiles gain their maximum DC input voltage. The dashboard loads three.js when a scene exists.
